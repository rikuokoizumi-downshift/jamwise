2. The product in one paragraph

A teacher types a topic or pastes a lesson. JamWise gives back a one-minute song with lyrics on screen, built for that grade, in a genre that fits the subject. The teacher plays it on the projector, then the class practices inside the song: karaoke fill-in-the-blank, rhythm recall, quiz boards. Results land per student while there is still time to reteach. The library of reviewed jams means tomorrow's lesson is usually already there; generation covers the rest.

The line against every competitor. Kahoot, Blooket and Gimkit test recall. MagicSchool, Knowt and Suno make songs. Flocabulary has songs and activities but one genre, 2004 video, and $11.50 a month. Only JamWise plays the game inside the music. That mechanic is the thing to protect in 1.0 and the thing to extend in 2.0.

5. Release 1.0 in one page

A closed beta for the teachers who already asked. Free, teachers only, web only. Five things have to work.

  1. Get in. Sign up, verify, sign in. Staging and production.
  2. Find a jam. A reviewed, rights-cleared library you can search by topic and filter by subject, grade and standard, with the real count on the site.
  3. Make a jam. Type a topic or paste lesson text, get a song with lyrics on screen, review it, play games on it. Three a month. Audio and lyrics only; no generated video for custom jams in 1.0.
  4. Play. Projector mode, and five games that pass the quality bar, at least two of them played inside the audio.
  5. Run a class. Create a class, students join by code with no personal info, assign a jam and a game, see results per student.

Plus the things that make it safe to let strangers in: a site that tells the truth, a current privacy policy, CI that merges, a seeded staging, one QA, and analytics events on the six measures.

Capacity, honestly. Seven weeks from Oct 27 to Dec 11, one of them Thanksgiving. Andi is finishing the Sketchie handoff. Nedi is shared across projects. Jaypal is on the pipeline. That is roughly one and a half engineers on the app. 1.0 is sized to that. Anything added to 1.0 after Nov 6 pushes something else out, and the PO will say which.
The calendar problem. Most US schools break around Dec 18 to 19. Invites on Dec 14 give teachers three or four school days with the product. Plan for it: beta invites and onboarding in December, real classroom usage and the first measures in the week of Jan 5. If week 1 unblocks slip past Nov 6, the honest date is Jan 5 invites, and the PO will call that by Nov 9, not in December.

Quality bar, draft for Nedi to rewrite

  • Playable state in under 3 seconds on school Wi-Fi, or a clear error with retry.
  • Instructions fit one screen; a Grade 3 student can start without the teacher explaining.
  • No dead ends. Every state has a way forward or out.

10. Release 2.0 in one page

The release a school buys. Flocabulary's whole lesson sequence, better video, games by age with live play on students' own devices, a price, a school plan, and the paperwork a district asks for before it signs.

  1. The full sequence. Video, vocab cards, vocab games, Break the Beat, Read and Respond, exit quiz, Verse Lab, discuss mode. Every step Flocabulary sells, plus the audio mechanic they cannot.
  2. Video at scale. Jaypal's pipeline renders the library and every reviewed custom jam, by video type and age group, with QA per stage.
  3. Games by age, live. Nedi's full set: singing and tapping modes for K-2, boards and sprints for 3-5, join-code live multiplayer with leaderboard for 6-8. Teacher-authored question sets. Ten or more games, all at the bar.
  4. Money. Credit-based Premium, Stripe, annual plan. School plan with admin dashboard, seats, teacher roster, usage. A published starting price for schools.
  5. Trust. District privacy pack, FERPA posture, SDPC agreement, accessibility statement, captions on everything, keyboard and screen-reader play.
  6. Plumbing teachers expect. Google Classroom, then Clever and ClassLink with the school plan. Public library preview for the signed-out visitor.
  7. Parents. Family accounts, child profiles, playlists. Last, because the school motion pays for it.

Target: set after two weeks of real beta usage (week of Jan 12). Working assumption is March 2027. Entry criteria in section 12.

11. Release 2.0: epics and features

Feature level with the stories that define each epic. Detailed stories get written when the epic enters Planning, with beta data in hand.

E11. Games by age and live play

Owner: Nedi. Depends on Nedi's games-by-age proposal (due week 1 of 1.0).

  • F11.1 Ten or more games, each tagged to grade bands, each at the bar. Beat Builder fixed or replaced. Crossword Craze and Lyric Scramble built or cut.
  • F11.2 K-2 modes that work on a shared screen with no reading: sing-along scoring, tap-the-beat, picture matching.
  • F11.3 Live mode (Grades 6-8, Kahoot-shaped so no teacher has to learn it): teacher starts a session, students join by code on their own devices, synchronous questions, speed bonus, leaderboard on the projector, results to the teacher instantly.
  • F11.4 Teacher-authored question sets: ten questions on any jam, run in live mode or assigned. Nedi's estimate: about a week after live mode exists.
  • F11.5 Student-side streaks and badges per class, no cross-class social features (privacy policy).
  • F11.6 Keyboard and screen-reader play; non-audio path for Lyric Lockdown and Rhythm Recall (FR-20).
US11.1 As a Grade 7 teacher, I want my class to play on their phones with a leaderboard on the board, so that the room competes the way it already does on Kahoot.
Done when: 30 students join by code in under a minute; the leaderboard updates within a second; the teacher has per-student results at the end with no grading.
US11.2 As a kindergarten teacher, I want a game my class can play from the carpet with no devices, so that the youngest rooms get the same energy.
Done when: at least two K-2 modes run fully on the shared screen with teacher-controlled input.

E12. Video at scale

Owner: Jaypal · Manuel for cost and vendor decisions

  • F12.1 Pipeline stages productised: transcript, story, shots, characters, keyframes, image-to-motion, lip-sync, cut, with automated QA per stage and a human review before publish.
  • F12.2 Four video types selectable per jam: concept, journey, process, personalised hero. Age groups 3-6 and 7-12 (and 13-14 for Grade 8).
  • F12.3 Every library jam re-rendered; every reviewed custom jam gets a video within a bounded time, with the audio-only version playable immediately.
  • F12.4 Cost per video logged; a per-jam cost ceiling; batch rendering off-peak.
  • F12.5 Character and style consistency across a subject so the library looks like one product, not 250 commissions.
  • F12.6 Rights position for generated visuals documented per vendor.
US12.1 As a teacher, I want the custom jam I made yesterday to have a video today, so that it looks like the rest of the library on the projector.
Done when: reviewed custom jam to published video within the bounded time, with a notification; audio-only never blocked while waiting.

E13. The full lesson sequence

Owner: Andi (surface) · Manuel (content generation) · Rikuo (pedagogy)

  • F13.1 Vocab cards: term, definition, part of speech, example sentence from the lyrics, image from the video, synonyms. Assignable.
  • F13.2 Break the Beat: timestamped multiple-choice on the jam with lyric-line evidence; one open-ended prompt for Grade 3+.
  • F13.3 Read and Respond: a short reading passage generated with each jam, reviewed with it, with detail, inference and main-idea questions.
  • F13.4 Exit quiz: plain ten-question auto-graded quiz for teachers who want the quiet version.
  • F13.5 Verse Lab: students write a verse to the jam's beat using its vocabulary; rhyme suggestions; teacher sees it first; same safety gate as custom jams; never public.
  • F13.6 Discuss mode in projector mode: pause points with a question on screen.
  • F13.7 Differentiation: assign any step to a group, not just the class.
US13.1 As a teacher who left Flocabulary, I want every step I used to run to exist here, so that switching costs me nothing.
Done when: each row of section 4 is Exists and a former Flocabulary teacher in the beta confirms the sequence covers their routine.

  • F14.2 Premium as credits: a monthly price buys credits; generation, video render and live sessions have published credit costs. Annual plan.
  • F14.3 Stripe checkout, invoices, cancel any time, 14-day trial decided then, not promised now.
  • F14.4 Published starting price for a school plan, near Gimkit group and Kahoot EDU anchors (competitive analysis priority 7).
  • F14.5 Price test: a lower annual Premium against the $59.88 anchor several competitors use.

E15. School plan

Owner: Andi · Manuel. The tier Manuel's partners sell.

  • F15.1 School account with seats; head teacher or admin invites teachers; teachers keep their own classes.
  • F15.2 Admin dashboard: active teachers, jams played, games run, results coverage by grade, generations used.
  • F15.3 Centralised billing, purchase order path, one invoice.
  • F15.4 Rostering: Google Classroom first; Clever and ClassLink with the school plan.
  • F15.5 Usage and outcomes export, de-identified by default (FR-23).
  • F15.6 Pilot support: two or three partner schools on a usage-and-outcomes pilot designed to meet ESSA Tier III (competitive analysis priority 7).
US15.1 As a head teacher, I want to buy once and roll it out to my teachers, so that I am not asking each of them to expense $7.99.
Done when: one purchase, N seats, teachers invited by email, admin sees who is using it.
